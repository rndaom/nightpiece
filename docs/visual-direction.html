Game

Nightpiece

Called Mortmain, Old French for “dead hand”, when this sheet was drawn.

A séance held in a concrete chapel. Two unseen players reach in with hands made of night, and the stone they move is alive and a little afraid of them.

Plan

The top-down camera, ivory to move, a knight under consideration.

A square the piece can move to A capture The piece would be lost here Threads mark the piece a hand is considering

  • The hand is the star

    Every move is a performance by a pair of spectral hands. They arrive as an outline, fill with night, close around the actual shape of the piece, and let go with a flourish.

  • Stone that is alive

    Pieces are small brutalist towers of stacked blocks held together by light. The seams breathe. One glowing aperture works as an eye and follows the hand.

  • Light carries the rules

    Legal squares, threats, paths and payoffs are drawn on the slab in flat, hard-edged light, so a beginner can read the game without opening a menu.

Schedule of finishes

Six materials. Nothing else appears in the room.

Elevations

The six pieces at eye level, measured in board squares. Sides alternate to show both stones.

Each piece is a stack of blocks with glowing seams, so it can breathe, flinch, lean and break apart without a face or limbs. Every silhouette differs at the top, where a hand and a top-down camera both read it.

Detail: the hand

How a spectral hand enters, holds and leaves.

  1. Arrive150 msFast, with a smear on the leading edge. Then it waits over the board for as long as the player thinks.
  2. Reach400 ms, slow inThe target square brightens a quarter second before the hand commits. Fingers open wider before they close.
  3. Close80 msA grip shaped to the piece: fingertips on a pawn's head, a wrap around a rook, a pinch under a knight's overhang.
  4. Lift250 msThe piece leaves the slab a beat after the hand rises, so it reads as heavy.
  5. Carry450 to 900 ms by distanceAn arc, the wrist leading and the piece swinging a few degrees behind. The room dims one stop.
  6. Set200 ms, slow outStone meets stone. Neighbouring squares lift and settle in a ring.
  7. Release100 msFingers spring open, overshoot, and settle.
  8. Withdraw350 ms, snap outA slow start and a quick exit. Fill fades first, outline last.
  • Light moves before the limb does.
  • Slow in, snap out. No linear motion anywhere a viewer can see it.
  • While idle the hands hover, drum their fingers and flex.
  • The opponent's hands are always visible across the board, doing what that player is doing.

Atmosphere

The room follows the engine's reading of the position.

CalmCool light from the oculus, warm candles at the corners. Pieces breathe slowly.
SharpCandles gutter, the shaft of light narrows and turns teal, haze thickens. The drone rises a tone.
CheckViolet fog at floor level. Everything but the king and its attacker drops a stop.
MateThe slab breaks into flying plates around a single pillar of light. The loser's stones go dark.

Everything on this sheet is original. The reference was studied for its vocabulary: silhouette hands with a drawn edge, flat lit squares, stone chess pieces, mint and ivory light, violet for the decisive moment. No models, textures, audio, logos, names or likenesses from Deadlock are used, and the brutalist chapel, the stacked pieces, the threads and the two-light scheme are this project's own.
